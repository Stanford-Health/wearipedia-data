# Use the Health app on your iPhone or iPad

The Health app gathers health data from your iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch,
and apps that you already use, so you can view all your progress in one
convenient place.

<img
src="https://cdsassets.apple.com/live/7WUAS350/images/ios-apps/built-in-apps/ios9-health-app-icon.png"
class="gb-image" style="max-width: min(100%,80px);" width="196"
height="196" alt="Health app icon" />

Health automatically counts your steps, walking, and running distances.
And, if you have an Apple Watch, it automatically tracks your Activity
data. You can also enter information into a Health category or get data
from any of your favorite apps or devices that are compatible with
Health.

## Set up your Health Profile

1.  On your iPhone or iPad, open the Health app.

2.  If you're on your iPhone, tap the Summary tab, then tap your
    <span class="inline-icon gb-inline-icon">profile picture<img
    src="https://cdsassets.apple.com/live/7WUAS350/images/ios/ios13-health-account-icon.png"
    class="gb-inline-icon-image" style="max-width: 22px" width="88"
    height="88" alt="No alt supplied for Image" /></span> in the
    upper-right corner. If you're on your iPad, tap Profile in the
    upper-left corner.

3.  Tap Health Details, then tap Edit.

4.  Add your information, like your date of birth and blood type.

    <img
    src="https://cdsassets.apple.com/live/7WUAS350/images/health/ios-17-iphone-14-pro-health-profile-edit-health-details.png"
    class="gb-image" style="max-width: min(100%,320px);" width="640"
    height="1302"
    alt="The Health Details screen on iPhone which shows Health Profile information such as Date of Birth and Blood Type." />

5.  Tap Done.

6.  On your iPhone, you can also set up your
    <a href="/en-us/105072" class="gb-anchor">Medical ID</a> or register
    to be an organ donor from your profile.

Your Apple Watch uses your personal information to calculate how many
calories you burn and more. You can choose to share this information
with other apps and it's kept up to date across all your devices.

After setting up your Health Profile, you can review your Health
Checklist initially from the Summary tab on your iPhone. The Health
Checklist lets you see and enable available health features. You can
also tap your profile picture in the upper-right, then tap Health
Checklist to access it.

## Pin the health data most important to you

1.  On your iPhone or iPad, open the Health app.

2.  If you're on your iPhone, tap the Summary tab. If you're on your
    iPad, <span class="inline-icon gb-inline-icon">tap sidebar<img
    src="https://cdsassets.apple.com/live/7WUAS350/images/inline-icons/ipados-17-ipad-split-screen-menu-icon.png"
    class="gb-inline-icon-image" style="max-width: 28px" width="100"
    height="78" alt="No alt supplied for Image" /></span>, then tap
    Summary.

3.  Tap Edit next to Pinned.

4.  To add or remove a category from your Pinned list, tap it.

5.  When you're finished, tap Done.

Your Pinned list shows how you're doing in each health category that
day, like Mindful Minutes or Steps. To see more information about a
category, tap it. If you need to manually add health details from
another app, learn how to
<a href="/en-us/105072" class="gb-anchor">manage Health data on your
iPhone, iPad, or Apple Watch</a>.

## Know how your health changes over time

1.  On your iPhone or iPad, open the Health app.

2.  If you're on your iPhone, tap the Summary tab. If you're on your
    iPad, <span class="inline-icon gb-inline-icon">tap sidebar<img
    src="https://cdsassets.apple.com/live/7WUAS350/images/inline-icons/ipados-17-ipad-split-screen-menu-icon.png"
    class="gb-inline-icon-image" style="max-width: 28px" width="100"
    height="78" alt="No alt supplied for Image" /></span>, then tap
    Summary.

3.  Scroll down to Highlights. Highlights show your Health over time, so
    you can see how you're doing overall.

    <img
    src="https://cdsassets.apple.com/live/7WUAS350/images/health/ios-17-iphone-14-pro-health-summary-highlights.png"
    class="gb-image" style="max-width: min(100%,320px);" width="640"
    height="1302"
    alt="An iPhone showing health highlights such as Active Energy and AFib History data over time." />

4.  Tap a highlight to see more about that category or tap Show All
    Highlights at the bottom of the list.

You can also receive notifications for new trends in your health data or
for other features such as Cycle Tracking or Mental Wellbeing. If you're
on your iPhone, tap the Summary tab, then tap your profile picture in
the upper-right corner. If you're on your iPad, tap Profile in the
upper-left corner. Under Features, tap Notifications and turn on any
notifications that interest you.

## Choose which apps share information with Health

1.  On your iPhone or iPad, open the Health app.

2.  If you're on your iPhone, tap the Summary tab, then tap your profile
    picture in the upper-right corner. If you're on your iPad, tap
    Profile in the upper-left corner.

3.  Under Privacy, tap Apps. You can see apps that you already own that
    are compatible with Health. If you don't see an app, it might not be
    compatible.

4.  Tap an app and turn on the health categories that you want that app
    to track.

If you're using multiple apps to track different things, the Health app
can keep it all in one place.
<a href="/en-us/108779" class="gb-anchor">Manage the data that's added
to Health app</a>.

Apple Watch apps can also read and write Health data. You can manage
them directly on your Apple Watch: Go to Settings \> Health \> Apps.
