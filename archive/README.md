# Archive

To determine the privacy and security performances of wearable devices, we extracted information from publicly available resources. Most documents are non-peer reviewed and provided by the companies themselves but serve as legally binding documents [[1](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/topics/protecting-consumer-privacy-security/privacy-security-enforcement)]. Others are non-peer reviewed articles, blog posts, or third-party plug ins.

There also were some peer reviewed articles that were used to determine the metrics for privacy and security.

- Abbott: https://doi.org/10.1177/1932296816681585
- Apple: https://doi.org/10.1080/15265161.2010.494215
- Apple: https://doi.org/10.1044/1092-4388(2010/09-0283)
- Apple: https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.2542
- Fitbit: https://doi.org/10.1145/1592761.1592778
- Fitbit: https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocv165
- Fitbit: https://doi.org/10.1145/2370216.2370253
- Whoop: https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocv165
